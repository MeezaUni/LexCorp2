Remote access to internal systems

Internal systems such as LexCorp2 are never published on the internet. From outside the company network you connect through the company VPN, and every connection asks you to sign in with your company account.

1 Sign in to get your VPN profile

You only need to do this once per device. For this demo, the company account is played by a Google account, and only approved accounts can download the profile.

Loading the sign-in button…

Signed in as . Your download should start automatically.

2 Install the VPN client

  • Windows: the OpenVPN Community client, version 2.6 or later ("OpenVPN GUI"), from openvpn.net/community-downloads. It's free. (OpenVPN Connect only partly supports the sign-in step.)
  • macOS: Tunnelblick 4 or later.

Then right-click the OpenVPN icon in the taskbar tray, choose Import file…, and pick lexcorp2-remote.ovpn.

3 Connect, every time

  1. Right-click the OpenVPN icon and choose Connect.
  2. Your browser opens and asks you to sign in with your company account (Google, for the demo).
  3. If your account is approved, the VPN connects. If not, the connection is refused.
  4. Open the LexCorp2 address you were given, and sign in to LexCorp2 as usual. Its own sign-in and factor checks still apply: the VPN only gets you to the door.

How it works

  1. Portal (this page): proves who you are with the company login, then hands out the VPN profile. The profile alone isn't enough to get in.
  2. VPN (OpenVPN Community Edition + openvpn-auth-oauth2): at every connection it sends your browser to the company login and accepts the connection only for approved accounts. Clients that can't do the browser sign-in are refused.
  3. Inside: a connected device can reach only the LexCorp2 web address, over HTTPS. Nothing else on the network is reachable through the VPN, and your own internet traffic doesn't use it.
  4. LexCorp2: its own sign-in, per-action factors, watermarking and audit trail apply as normal.

In a real deployment

At an organisation that runs its own employee or supplier portal (for example a defence manufacturer's existing sign-in portal), this download would sit behind that portal's login, and the VPN would ask the organisation's own identity provider (for example Microsoft Entra ID, AD FS or Keycloak) instead of Google. That's the same open standard (OpenID Connect) with different settings, so nothing in LexCorp2 changes.

This demo site is deliberately separate: LexCorp2 doesn't trust it or share accounts with it. It only illustrates where the VPN profile would come from.
